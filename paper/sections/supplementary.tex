\section{Additional Results}
\label{sec:supp_results}

\paragraph{Candidate Selection.}
\label{sec:supp_coverage}
\Cref{tab:coverage} separates two sources of error on part queries: choosing the wrong candidate, and having no good
candidate in the pool. On PARTONOMY, an oracle that always picks the candidate with the highest IoU reaches $71.0$
gIoU, against $49.4$ for \ours{} and $42.0$ for SAM\,3's top-ranked instance. \ours{} closes $26\%$ of the gap between
SAM\,3's ranking and the oracle, and its pick is better on $25.9\%$ of the queries and worse on $11.5\%$. On
InstructPart, \ours{} is $0.8$ gIoU above SAM\,3's ranking and closes $7\%$ of the gap, and the two picks win about
equally often ($11.3\%$ and $11.8\%$). On both benchmarks the oracle remains well above \ours{}, so a better
selection alone could improve grounding without generating new masks. The lower part of \cref{tab:coverage} shows
where the masks come from. The best mask in the pool comes from the point grid for $58.8\%$ of the PARTONOMY queries
and $52.3\%$ of the InstructPart queries, and from the SAM\,3 text instances for the rest, so both sources are needed.
\ours{} itself picks a text instance more often ($64.7\%$ and $72.0\%$), mostly one for the part phrase.

\subsection{Larger Backbones}
\label{sec:supp_backbones}
\Cref{tab:backbones} repeats the protocol of \cref{tab:controlled} with Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-32B. These rows are
references only and are not part of the same-size comparison. At 8B, \ours{} is trained on the same data with the
same pool, without the tree-relation terms. It is ahead of Box\,+\,SAM\,3 at 8B on every part benchmark except
InstructPart ($-0.1$), and ahead on gRefCOCO by $7.8$ and $8.7$ gIoU. It also rejects $64.8$ and $58.7$ points more
no-target expressions, and it is $1.0$ point behind on GSEval All. With the 32B MLLM, Box\,+\,SAM\,3 improves part
grounding only modestly. It reaches $33.2$ on PACO parts, $37.1$ on PPS-116 and $49.3$ on PartImageNet, against
$47.5$, $40.6$ and $50.7$ for \ours{} at 4B. It rejects $44.7\%$ and $43.8\%$ of the no-target expressions, against
$94.0\%$ and $90.9\%$ for \ours{}. It is above \ours{} at 4B on InstructPart ($70.5$ vs.\ $68.2$) and GSEval All
($72.7$ vs.\ $70.0$).

\subsection{Further Analyses}
\label{sec:supp_further}

\paragraph{Baseline prompts on reasoning queries.}
In \cref{tab:reasoning}, each baseline receives its own question prompt. \Cref{tab:reason_prompts} compares this
with the phrase template of \cref{tab:controlled}. The prompt changes each baseline by at most $2.0$ gIoU, far less
than the margin of \ours{} over Box\,+\,SAM\,3 on InstructPart ($11.2$ and $8.5$ gIoU), so the conclusions of
\cref{tab:reasoning} do not depend on the choice of prompt.

\input{tables/tabB_reason_prompts}

\paragraph{Multi-target referring.}
In the main comparison every method returns one mask per query. gRefCOCO also contains expressions that refer to
several objects, so \cref{tab:multitarget} lets every method return several masks and scores their union. \ours{}
returns all candidates with $\sigma(s_v)\geq t$ (\cref{sec:impl}). Under this protocol, \ours{} has the highest gIoU
($72.3$ and $65.2$) and N-acc ($89.6$ and $86.0$) on both test splits, while Box\,+\,SAM\,3 with all boxes has the
higher cIoU on testB ($58.9$ vs.\ $58.3$). SAM\,3 with all text instances rejects almost as often on testA ($89.3$)
but grounds the targets much less accurately ($44.3$ gIoU). These are the gRefCOCO numbers reported for \ours{} in
\cref{tab:published}.

\input{tables/tabC_multitarget}

\paragraph{Tree-relation terms.}
\Cref{tab:tree_ablation} removes the tree-relation attention bias and the family loss from the full model (row (e) of
\cref{tab:ablation}). Grounding changes little: the full model is higher on GSEval parts ($+2.3$), PPS-116 ($+0.7$)
and InstructPart ($+1.0$), lower on gRefCOCO testB ($-1.2$), and within $0.4$ points elsewhere. The clearest effect
is on rejection, where the tree terms add $4.1$ and $5.0$ N-acc. The full model was also the better of the two on
validation ($85.6$ vs.\ $85.0$ selection success on PACO), which is how it was chosen.

\input{tables/tabD_tree_ablation}

\paragraph{GSEval by expression category.}
\Cref{tab:gseval_categories} splits GSEval by the category of the referring expression. \ours{} is best on part
expressions, $13.9$ gIoU above Box\,+\,SAM\,3. It is behind Box\,+\,SAM\,3 on single objects ($-4.0$), multiple
objects ($-4.3$) and stuff ($-5.7$), and on multiple objects Sa2VA is best. These three categories hold $88\%$ of the
expressions, which explains the gap of $2.3$ points on GSEval All in \cref{tab:controlled}.

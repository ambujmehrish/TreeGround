\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
Our target task is part grounding, so most of our benchmarks ask for a part. We use six: PARTONOMY, the part
subset of GSEval, PACO, Pascal-Part-116, PartImageNet and InstructPart. To see whether a model built for parts
still handles whole objects, we add object queries from PACO and Pascal-Part, the full GSEval set, and the
referring expressions of gRefCOCO (testA and testB). gRefCOCO also contains expressions with no target in the
image, which lets us measure rejection. Beyond named parts, we test reasoning queries, where the target is
described by its use rather than its name, on the instructions of InstructPart and on ReasonSeg. Finally, we test
the masks as input to image editing on GEdit-Bench and ImgEdit-Bench. All numbers are on test splits. Our model is
trained on the training splits of PACO, Pascal-Part and gRefCOCO; these columns are marked as in-domain in the
tables, and every other benchmark is zero-shot.

\paragraph{One mask per query.}
A request for a part refers to one region, so every method answers with at most one mask per query. For a method
that returns several instances, we keep the one with its highest score, and we keep no mask when that score is
below the method's released threshold. Our model returns its top-scoring candidate, with the rejection threshold
$\tau$ chosen on the validation split of gRefCOCO and used only there. This rule is the same for all rows of a
table, which is what makes them comparable. Results with several masks per expression are reported separately
(Tab.~\ref{tab:multitarget}).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report gIoU, the mean over queries of the IoU between the predicted and the ground-truth mask. On gRefCOCO a
no-target expression scores $1$ for an empty answer and $0$ otherwise, and N-acc is the share of no-target
expressions answered with no mask. For reasoning queries we also report cIoU, the total intersection divided by
the total union over all queries. For every comparison in the main table we test significance with a paired
bootstrap over images ($10{,}000$ resamples).

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against two kinds of systems, and we run all of them ourselves on the same queries and with the same
scoring code. The first kind uses no MLLM: SAM\,3 prompted with the text of the query, and Grounded-SAM\,2, which
turns the boxes of an open-vocabulary detector into masks. The second kind uses an MLLM. Sa2VA generates the mask
with its own decoder, and Box\,+\,SAM\,3 lets the MLLM predict a box that SAM\,3 turns into a mask. To separate the
method from the size of the language model, every MLLM-based row uses the same backbone as ours, Qwen3-VL-4B.
Results with larger backbones are in the appendix (Tab.~\ref{tab:backbones}) and serve as references only. We also
compare with numbers published by other systems under the same protocols (Tab.~\ref{tab:published}).

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Our model combines Qwen3-VL-4B with a frozen SAM\,3. It adds $64.2$M trainable parameters, $1.2\%$ of the system:
LoRA of rank $16$ on the language model, the mask-token MLP and the selection head. The pool has about $260$
candidates per query ($G=32$, $N_g=256$, $k=3$). We train for $12{,}000$ steps with a batch of $32$ images on a mix
of PACO, Pascal-Part and gRefCOCO. The checkpoint, the read-out layer $\ell^{\star}$ and $\tau$ are chosen on
validation data; nothing is tuned on a test split. All remaining settings are listed in the appendix
(Sec.~\ref{sec:impl}).
